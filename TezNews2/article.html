'Not an era of war': EAM outlines India's stance on global conflicts, stresses zero tolerance on terror

External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar addressed world leaders at a high-level security forum, reaffirming India's commitment to diplomacy while drawing a firm line against cross-border terrorism.

Priya Sharma Senior Correspondent, New Delhi
📅 June 11, 2026 ⏲ 5 min read
Share: WhatsApp X / Twitter Facebook 🔗 Copy Link

NEW DELHI — External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar on Wednesday delivered a pointed address at the Global Security Forum, declaring that the current era "is not an era of war" while simultaneously reaffirming India's absolute zero-tolerance stance toward cross-border terrorism. The speech, delivered before an audience of over 40 foreign dignitaries, was widely seen as a calibrated response to the ongoing instability along India's northern and western borders.

"We believe that diplomacy must always be the first resort, not the last," Jaishankar said, his remarks drawing sustained applause from the assembled delegates. "But let there be no ambiguity — India will not accept, negotiate with, or accommodate terrorism in any form, from any direction."

"We believe that diplomacy must always be the first resort, not the last. But let there be no ambiguity — India will not accept, negotiate with, or accommodate terrorism in any form." — S. Jaishankar, External Affairs Minister

The address came against the backdrop of heightened tensions following a series of ceasefire violations reported along the Line of Control over the past two weeks. Defence analysts have noted a marked increase in cross-border activity, prompting back-channel communications between New Delhi and Islamabad through Gulf-based intermediaries.

Jaishankar touched on India's evolving foreign policy posture, emphasising the country's growing role as a "Vishwabandhu" — a friend of the world — while stressing that such friendship came with clear expectations of reciprocity on matters of security. He cited India's recent role in facilitating ceasefire talks in Eastern Europe and its contributions to UN peacekeeping missions as evidence of the country's commitment to multilateralism.

India's Strategic Patience Running Thin

Behind the measured diplomatic language, senior government officials who spoke to TezNews on condition of anonymity indicated that India's patience with continued provocations was not unlimited. "There is a framework within which dialogue operates," one official said. "When that framework is repeatedly violated, the calculus changes."

The minister also addressed the broader geopolitical environment, taking aim — without naming specific nations — at what he called "state-sponsored destabilisation strategies" that undermined regional peace. The reference was broadly interpreted by diplomatic observers as directed at both Pakistan and China.

Opposition Responds

The opposition was quick to react, with Congress spokesperson Pawan Khera calling the speech "strong on rhetoric but weak on actionable outcomes." Khera demanded that the government table a white paper on the current border situation before Parliament's upcoming monsoon session.

The BJP, however, rallied behind the minister. Party spokesperson Sambit Patra described the address as "a masterclass in strategic communication," arguing that Jaishankar had successfully conveyed India's red lines without unnecessarily escalating tensions.

The Forum is scheduled to continue through Thursday, with sessions on maritime security in the Indo-Pacific and the regulation of emerging dual-use technologies on the agenda. Jaishankar is expected to hold bilateral meetings with the foreign ministers of Japan, Australia and Germany on the sidelines.
